Question

Golden or Gilded?

Mark Twain, 1873. A name for a time.

In 1873, Mark Twain wrote a book about America. He called it The Gilded Age. He did not call it The Golden Age. Look at the two rings. They look the same, but are they the same inside?

Big question

Why “gilded,” not “golden”?

Dictionary

gilded = covered with a thin layer of gold

1873년에 마크 트웨인은 미국에 대한 책을 쓰고 제목을 『The Gilded Age』라고 지었어요. ‘The Golden Age’가 아니었죠. 두 반지를 보세요. 똑같아 보이지만, 속도 똑같을까요?

gilded는 ‘금을 얇게 입힌’이라는 뜻이에요. golden은 속까지 금이지만, gilded는 겉에만 금이 있어요. 왜 트웨인은 그 시대를 ‘gilded’라고 불렀을지 모둠과 이야기해 보세요.

O. Henry's Life

A bank, a prison, a new name. Then New York.

1Texas

He worked on a ranch and in a drugstore.

2The bank

He worked at a bank as a teller.

3Prison

Stories in magazines, and a new name.

4New York

55 Irving Place, near Gramercy Park.

O. Henry's real name was William Sydney Porter. He was born in North Carolina in 1862. In Texas, he worked on a ranch, in a drugstore, and at a bank as a teller. People said he took money from the bank, so he went to prison.

While he was in prison, he sent his stories to magazines. In 1899, a magazine printed one of his stories with the name “O. Henry.” In 1902, he moved to New York. He lived near Gramercy Park, at 55 Irving Place. Many people say he wrote The Gift of the Magi there in 1905.

감옥에 있는 동안 그는 이야기를 써서 잡지사에 보냈어요. 1899년, 한 잡지가 그의 이야기를 ‘O. Henry’라는 이름으로 실었어요. 1902년 그는 뉴욕으로 이사해 그래머시 파크 근처 어빙 플레이스 55번지에 살았어요. 많은 사람이 그가 1905년 그곳에서 「The Gift of the Magi」를 썼다고 말해요.

Who is worth knowing? Two answers.

400

4,000,000
O. Henry, 1906

Every New Yorker had a story.

In New York, O. Henry walked the streets and listened to shop girls, clerks, and waiters. A rich man, Ward McAllister, said only about 400 people in New York really mattered. O. Henry did not agree. About four million people lived in New York, and he believed every one of them had a story.

TT · Time Traveler

Two Homes

New York, 1905. Two worlds in one city.

Fifth Avenue mansionThe 400
TenementThe four million

On Fifth Avenue, rich families lived in big stone mansions. They had many rooms, servants, and ballrooms for parties. Not far away, poor families lived in tenements. A whole family often lived in one or two small rooms. Some rooms had no windows, and people shared a toilet in the hall.

Think and share

What is one difference between the two homes?

5번가의 부자 가족들은 커다란 돌집, 대저택에 살았어요. 방이 많았고, 하인과 파티를 여는 무도회장도 있었어요. 멀지 않은 곳의 가난한 가족들은 공동주택(tenement)에 살았어요. 한 가족이 작은 방 한두 칸에 사는 일이 많았고, 창문 없는 방도 있었으며, 복도의 화장실을 여럿이 같이 썼어요.

McAllister counted 400. O. Henry counted everyone.

400
From the preface · The Four Million, 1906
Not very long ago some one invented the assertion that there were only “Four Hundred” people in New York City who were really worth noticing. But a wiser man has arisen—the census taker—and his larger estimate of human interest has been preferred in marking out the field of these little stories of the “Four Million.”
